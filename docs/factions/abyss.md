# Abyss Faction – Domain of Death and Corruption

> Source: Realm of Nexus RTS Design Bible, Volume 1 (Core Faction Mechanics). Sheet drafted Aug 10, 2025.

## Faction Summary (Playstyle & Strategic Identity)

The Abyss faction embodies relentless attrition and dark sorcery. It plays as a slow-building powerhouse that harvests souls and spreads corruption across the battlefield to gradually overwhelm opponents. Early on, Abyss forces are fragile and reliant on necromancy and sacrifice, but they grow in strength with every casualty. Enemies that fall in battle become fuel for the Abyss war machine – death is never wasted. The faction's strategic identity is one of inevitability: through patience, area denial, and raising the dead, the Abyss grinds down foes. As corruption spreads, the Abyss army gains map control, debuffing enemies on tainted ground and empowering its own undead legions. By late-game, an Abyss commander can unleash void-infused abominations and endless undead hordes, sacrificing lesser minions to empower elites. This faction rewards players who plan for the long game, leveraging necrotic magic and bold sacrificial plays to eventually snowball into an unstoppable tide of darkness. However, an Abyss player must survive the early game's weakness and carefully manage their soul economy, or risk being snuffed out before their doom engine comes online.

## Main Base Upgrade Path (Tier 1 → Tier 3)

- **Tier 1 – Necropolis of Shades:** The starting Abyss base is a grim Necropolis that acts as a conduit for soul energy. It provides basic unit production and a limited corruption aura around it. *Unlock Condition:* Starting structure (no prerequisite). *Benefits:* Trains T1 workers and basic infantry; slowly spreads "blight" (corrupted ground) in a modest radius that slightly heals undead and withers living enemies.
- **Tier 2 – Profane Citadel:** The Necropolis can be upgraded into a Profane Citadel after accumulating a stockpile of souls and constructing a tech structure (e.g. Nightforge). *Unlock Condition:* Requires X Soul Essence (primary resource) and a Tier 1 tech building; a ritual sacrifice of a unit may be required to initiate the dark ascension. *Benefits:* Greatly expands build options – unlocks advanced unit structures (necromancer halls, flesh forges), allows a second hero to be summoned, and increases the radius and potency of ground corruption. Also boosts soul-harvest efficiency (yield from kills) domain-wide.
- **Tier 3 – Oblivion Spire:** The final base upgrade transforms the citadel into an Oblivion Spire, a towering nexus of Void power that fully embraces the Abyss's Void corruption. *Unlock Condition:* Requires Tier 2, a large sum of Soul Essence + Voidstone (secondary resource), and one Oblivion Shard (legendary catalyst) offered in a dark ritual. *Benefits:* Unlocks all elite units and ultimate technologies (e.g. void dragons, death knights), permits training the third hero, and grants a global passive "Shadow of the Void" (e.g. all enemy units on corrupted ground suffer reduced attack). The Oblivion Spire also massively increases corruption spread across the map and can even resurrect a limited number of fallen units automatically within its domain.

## Resource System (Economy of Souls and Corruption)

- **Primary Resource – Soul Essence:** The lifeblood of the Abyss economy, Soul Essence is harvested from the dead. Every enemy (or allied) unit that dies near Abyss forces yields spectral soul energy. Workers (Acolytes) can also channel at "Soul Wells" found on the map or at sacrificial altars to continuously generate Soul Essence, representing tribute of souls from the domain's populace. This resource is used for nearly everything – training basic undead, constructing buildings, and fueling necromantic spells. *Gathering:* Automatically collected from battlefields (via units or structures like Soul Wells) and by sacrificing one's own units in dedicated structures. *Usage:* Acts as currency to animate skeletons, research necrotic upgrades, and maintain the undeath infrastructure. High Soul Essence reserves accelerate Abyss growth, but if battles are scarce, Abyss must actively sacrifice lesser minions to keep the soul flow going.
- **Secondary Resource – Voidstone (Corrupted Aether):** Once the Void infiltrated the Abyss, pockets of corrupted crystal began forming. Voidstone shards are dark crystals imbued with Void energy, harvested from corrupted sites or created through foul alchemy. *Gathering:* Requires a Void Mine on tainted ground or conversion of Soul Essence at a Corruption Crucible – essentially condensing souls into solid Voidstone. *Usage:* Needed for advanced units, such as Void-touched abominations and heavier siege demons, and for Tier 2+ research (e.g. improving corruption spread, curse potency). Voidstone is also expended in ritual tech like global debuffs or plague spells. Managing Voidstone introduces a strategic choice: spend Soul Essence to produce more Voidstone for power spikes, or save souls for immediate army growth.
- **Legendary Catalyst – Oblivion Shard:** A fragment of the infamous Oblivion Stone, the Oblivion Shard is an extremely rare and potent catalyst. Only a Tier 3 Oblivion Spire or special world events can produce these shards (for instance, corrupting an ancient shrine or slaying a powerful mythical creature might yield one). *Gathering:* An Oblivion Shard might be granted after a costly ritual victory or periodically from a fully upgraded Spire that is channeling Void energy. *Usage:* Required for the most powerful Abyssal actions – for example, summoning the ultimate unit (such as a Void Lich King or an Apocalyptic Wurm), or unlocking the final tech like "Eclipse of Life" (which temporarily blankets the map in darkness). It might also be consumed by a hero's ultimate ability for game-changing effect. Because it's so scarce, deciding when and how to use an Oblivion Shard is a critical late-game decision for Abyss players.

## Heroes (Necromantic Champions of the Abyss)

### Warlord Tyvaris, the Grave Tyrant (Military Hero)

A once-mortal conqueror now sustained by necromancy, Tyvaris is a hulking melee champion who leads from the front. He excels at buffing allied undead and instilling terror in enemies.

- **Soulcleaver Blade:** Tyvaris strikes with his void-infused greatsword, dealing heavy damage to a target. If the target dies, its soul is instantly harvested and heals Tyvaris or nearby undead.
- **Dread Banner:** He raises a tattered war banner that radiates dread. Allied units in range gain increased attack and damage resistance, while enemy units have reduced morale and attack speed under its ominous influence.
- **Sacrificial Surge:** Tyvaris can execute one of his own lesser units to enrage himself and nearby troops – granting a temporary boost to movement and attack damage. This gruesome sacrifice also yields a burst of Soul Essence.
- **Aura of the Pale King (Passive):** The Warlord's presence spreads Abyssal corruption. He passively extends corrupted ground around him as he moves, and undead under his command periodically reanimate once after being killed (rising again as skeletons). This makes Tyvaris-led assaults very difficult to completely repel.

### Neratha Soulweaver (Economic/Support Hero)

An ancient necromancer crone from the Whisperless Sect, Neratha forgoes direct damage for bolstering the Abyss economy and army sustain. She is relatively weak in combat but invaluable behind the lines.

- **Harvest Soul:** Neratha siphons life-force from a target (unit or corpse) to produce a quick injection of Soul Essence. If used on a living enemy unit, it deals moderate damage over time; if the unit dies under this curse, it yields bonus resources.
- **Corruption Ward:** Places a profane totem on the ground that quickens corruption spread in an area and heals allied undead over time. The ward also reveals stealth units (through eerie whisperings) and slightly slows any enemy caught in its corruption field. Useful for fortifying areas or creeping forward with Abyss territory.
- **Dark Pact:** In a ritual of blood, Neratha can sacrifice a portion of her own health (or an allied unit's life) to greatly speed up production and research at a target structure for a short duration. For example, she can empower a Crypt to train units twice as fast or a forge to finish an upgrade instantly. This ability lets the Abyss hit sudden power spikes at the cost of life force.
- **Undying Devotion (Passive):** Her dark prayers bolster the resilience of the undead. All Abyss units near Neratha have a chance to negate fatal damage (avoiding death once every few minutes). This support aura makes initial waves of her army surprisingly durable, buying time for other mechanics to kick in.

### Malazar the Voidcaller (Magical/Utility Hero)

A Lich sorcerer who delved too deep into Void magic, Malazar is a ranged spellcaster with a suite of battlefield control and debilitation spells. His abilities exemplify the Abyss's mastery of fear and corruption.

- **Void Bolt:** Fires a crackling bolt of pure Void energy at range, splitting on impact. The primary target takes heavy damage, and the bolt then jumps to several nearby enemies as lesser shadow bolts, inflicting moderate damage and a brief silence (preventing spellcasting).
- **Nightmare Veil:** Malazar summons a shroud of darkness over a targeted area, creating a Miasma of fear. Enemies caught in the cloud see their vision greatly reduced and are afflicted by terror (minor chance to flee or suffer reduced control responsiveness). Allied undead inside the veil become invisible as long as they don't attack, allowing ambush tactics or retreats.
- **Nether Portal:** Ripping open a small portal to the Abyssal Void, Malazar instantly teleports a cluster of units (and himself) a short distance – useful for repositioning forces or bypassing walls. Alternatively, he can target an enemy cluster: instead of teleporting, the portal banishes those enemy units briefly (removing them from play for a second or two). This versatile spell provides high utility for both offense and escape.
- **Curse of Undeath (Ultimate):** Malazar places a lingering curse on a wide swath of the battlefield. Any enemy units that die under this curse immediately rise as lesser undead under Abyss control (e.g. fallen infantry become skeleton warriors temporarily). This ultimate can suddenly swing a battle of attrition, turning the enemy's losses into fodder for the Abyss. The curse lasts several seconds on all affected units and can flood the field with undead if a large clash occurs during its effect.

## Buildings & Production Structures (By Tech Tier)

### Tier 1 Buildings

- **Grim Crypt:** The basic unit barracks of the Abyss, where low-tier undead are animated. Trains Skeleton Warriors (melee fodder infantry) and Gravebow Archers (ranged skeletons). The Crypt uses Soul Essence to reassemble bones into troops.
- **Occult Den:** A small temple for cultists and basic spellcasters. Trains Acolytes (worker units that also can perform minor combat rituals) and Shadow Adepts (Tier 1 support unit with a life-drain attack). Also houses cheap upgrades for unit attack/armor via dark blessings.
- **Soul Well:** An economic structure that Acolytes can build on resource nodes (or in base to slowly generate souls). Acts as a drop-off point for Soul Essence and can be toggled to sacrifice a friendly unit to yield a burst of resources. Soul Wells on corrupted ground will spread that corruption further as they accumulate souls.

(Tier 1 also allows basic defensive walls, and the Necropolis itself serves as the initial drop-off/production point.)

### Tier 2 Buildings

- **Necromancer's Spire:** Trains Necromancers (spellcasting unit that raises zombies or casts curses) and Banshees (flying ghost units for harassment). Also researches advanced necromantic upgrades (e.g. increasing the number of skeletons a Necromancer can raise or improving undead movement speed on corrupted ground).
- **Flesh Forge (Abomination Pit):** A gruesome workshop where stitched abominations and siege engines of bone are created. Trains Flesh Abominations (tanky melee monsters) and Bone Catapults (siege artillery that lobs putrid skulls). Upgrades here can improve unit durability or unlock plague damage (damage-over-time on attacks).
- **Reliquary of Darkness:** A special support structure that enables the Relic Augmentation system. Abyss commanders can craft Dark Relics (weapons, armor, icons) using Soul Essence and Voidstone, and use them to customize their elite undead (for example, imbuing a relic with "Bloodflare Etchings" to create a vampiric effect on a summoned unit). The Reliquary doesn't train units directly, but it empowers existing ones. It is also the prerequisite building to upgrade to Tier 3, as one must prepare the Oblivion ritual here.
- **Corruption Crucible:** An economic/refining structure that converts raw Soul Essence into Voidstone slowly over time. It can also corrupt nearby neutral resources – for example, placed on a neutral crystal mine, it taints the crystals into Voidstone that only Abyss can use. Crucibles let Abyss deny resources to enemies while enhancing their secondary resource income.

### Tier 3 Buildings

- **Void Gate:** A sinister gate-like portal that can summon Void-spawned units from the beyond. Trains Void Knights (elite shock cavalry wreathed in shadow) and Nether Dragons (flying drakes exhaling corrupting breath). These units require both Soul Essence and Voidstone. The Void Gate may also passively spawn minor shades for free over time (small scouts or harassers), representing the thinning barrier between realms.
- **Cathedral of Decay:** The ultimate tech structure of the Abyss. Unlocks Tier 3 upgrades (e.g. global lifesteal aura for all undead, or immunity to morale for undead), and houses a super-ability (like channeling a realm-wide death fog if a hero uses the structure). The Cathedral can also summon the Abyss's legendary unit (with Oblivion Shard): for instance, the Deathlord, a one-of-a-kind uber-unit or temporary Avatar of Virexus. Only one such unit can exist at a time.
- Additionally, Tier 3 fortifies existing infrastructure: the base upgrade allows building Void Obelisks on corrupted land without workers, and most Tier 1/2 structures gain extra functions (like Crypts automatically raising a free skeleton from any corpse within range).

## Defensive Structures (Unique Abyssal Defenses)

- **Soul Obelisk:** The primary defensive tower. An obsidian obelisk adorned with screaming skulls that fires bolts of necrotic energy. These bolts leech life – damaging enemies and healing allied undead nearby. Longer range on corrupted ground; targets ground and air. Upgrades can allow the Obelisk to collect Soul Essence from each kill it makes.
- **Corruption Spire:** Does minimal direct damage but continuously expands a field of blight around itself. Enemies in the area suffer movement slow, armor reduction, and gradual HP decay. If multiple Spires' auras overlap, the corruption intensifies (stacking effect), forcing enemies to prioritize destroying them.
- **Bone Walls & Gate:** Barricades of fused bone and sinew that repair themselves over time by absorbing death energy. Any unit (friend or foe) that dies adjacent to a Bone Wall segment will partially heal that segment. Gates are fashioned from giant rib-cages and emit a short-range necrotic pulse if breached, momentarily cursing nearby enemies with weakness.
- **Plague Mine (Trap):** Hidden plague traps (invisible cysts or rune circles) that erupt in a cloud of plague when an enemy steps near. No immediate damage, but diseases enemy troops – reducing their attack and slowly sapping health for a duration. Ideal for ambushes or protecting flanks.

## Progression Flow (T1 → T3 Pacing and Strategy)

- **Early Game (Tier 1):** The Abyss begins cautiously. Field small squads of skeletons and cultists which are cheap but weak in straight fights. Avoid pitched battles early; focus on creeping and skirmishing to gather Soul Essence. Use Acolytes and Soul Wells to claim resource points and sacrifice a few expendable units to kick-start the soul economy. Spreading corruption is vital – build a Corruption Ward or fight around the Necropolis to create blighted ground. Harass the enemy with expendable units (even a pack of skeletons dying in the enemy worker line yields souls). Set up a Soul Obelisk or two and Bone Walls to buy time. Goal: survival and soul accumulation, reaching Tier 2 with a healthy stock of Soul Essence and at least one hero.
- **Mid Game (Tier 2):** The Abyss enters its power spike phase. Necromancers and Abominations significantly bolster army power and longevity. Unit reanimation and recursion kicks into high gear. Establish a Corruption Crucible for Voidstone and corrupt neutral resources. Push the corruption frontier outward with Corruption Spires or Neratha's wards so every forward position becomes a zone of weakness for the opponent. Mid-game armies revolve around durable Abominations soaking damage, swarms of skeletons clogging the field, and Necromancers or Malazar cursing from the backline. Leapfrog forward: corrupt an area, fight there, win through attrition, then move up. Time major Voidstone upgrades (e.g. Blight Plague) to swing battles.
- **Late Game (Tier 3):** Dominance and inevitability. Waves of augmented undead, supported by Void Knights and perhaps the summoned Deathlord, push inexorably into the enemy base. Soul Essence is plentiful from all the fighting. Max-level heroes are extremely impactful: Tyvaris makes the army nearly unkillable, Neratha maximizes production, Malazar's Curse of Undeath creates entire battalions from enemy dead. Full map corruption is possible. Use an Oblivion Shard decisively (summon the undead titan, or cast a realm-wide Eclipse). Remain methodical: advance siege units under cover of corruption, and use Void Gates to attack from multiple angles.

## Strengths & Weaknesses

### Strengths

- **Attritional Mastery:** Thrives in long battles – turns enemy losses into its gains through soul harvesting, raising dead, and snowballing numbers.
- **Recursive Unit Cycle:** Ability to reanimate fallen units and cheap expendable troops means Abyss armies are hard to truly wipe out.
- **Map Control via Corruption:** Spreading blight gives significant map control. Fighting on corrupted ground debuffs enemies and buffs Abyss units, creating "home turf" anywhere with setup.
- **Powerful Late-Game:** Fully teched, the Abyss fields some of the most powerful units and effects in the game.
- **Sacrificial Tactics:** Unique sacrificial strategies (killing a portion of the army to boost the rest or gain resource surges) offer unpredictable power spikes.

### Weaknesses

- **Slow Early Game:** Weak starting units and reliance on gathering souls make the Abyss vulnerable to early rushes.
- **Dependency on Death:** The economy depends on things dying. Opponents who avoid engagements, use hit-and-run, or deny corpses (fire, holy cleansing) can starve the Abyss.
- **Micromanagement & Timing:** Managing sacrifices, raising dead, and dual resources adds complexity; mistimed abilities can backfire.
- **Vulnerable to Burst Damage & Focus Fire:** Lower immediate HP/armor than equivalent-tier units; strong burst AoE or fire/holy damage can shatter an undead army before its sustain kicks in. Frail heroes like Neratha can be sniped.
- **Terrain/Speed Limitations:** Largely ground-based and slow. Struggles on large maps against multi-pronged attacks.
